1 - Screen-edge peek

Rationale. The buddy lives on the right edge of the screen, half out of frame, head turned toward your work with two feather-tip wings hooked over the edge: a bird peeking round a doorframe. The edge itself is the "window", so there is no tile, and position alone carries the state: it slides further in to nudge or celebrate, tucks to just eyes and a beak tip in focus, and sinks and droops for break. The step/timer chip floats beside it, so the character stays tiny and the information stays readable.

Honest weaknesses. Half the face is always hidden, so expressions are limited to eyes and beak (the sand cap and wings do much of the species work). It only works glued to a screen edge: it needs edge-snapping logic, multi-monitor handling, and must avoid covering scrollbars and window-close buttons on the right. The wings-gripping read is subtle at 100 px. A frameless transparent window only that wide also means the slide-in has to be done by moving the window or by oversizing it, which is an engineering cost.
