2 - Corner sitter (full little bird)

Rationale. The whole bird is visible, sitting in the bottom-right corner on top of the taskbar like a desk pet: one rounded egg-shaped body (head and body are a single shape) with the sand cap, the amber beak wedge, calm eyes, two pointed feather-tip wings hanging at its sides and two tiny amber feet. Having a body and feet is what makes it unmistakably a bird (the beak plus wings do the rest, and the dark collar is gone). The timer pill sits to its left on the same ground line, so the buddy reads as a small creature with a tiny status chip, not a widget. All motion is small: breathing, a glance toward your window, a hop to nudge, a bigger hop to celebrate. In focus it shrinks to 80%, closes its eyes and goes still.

Honest weaknesses.

  • Always-visible full body costs more screen space than a peeking head (about 150 x 160 px plus the pill) and the bottom-right corner is where notifications and tray popups land, so it can collide with them.
  • A pure front view with a one-shape body is cute but generic; the species cues are small (cap, beak, wing tips) and it can still read as a round chick at 90 px.
  • It has to sit on the taskbar/dock edge to feel "grounded", but taskbar height and position vary (left/top docks, auto-hide, multi-monitor), so the placement logic needs a per-OS offset.
  • The pill lives to the left, so the window needs to be about 230 px wide even though the bird is only about 110 px; the bubble adds height. The always-on-top window must be click-through in the empty areas.
  • Glancing at "your window" is faked with a fixed up-left look; it cannot really track what you are doing (and we do not want it to).
