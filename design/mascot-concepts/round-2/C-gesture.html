Plover, direction C: Gesture

A bird you recognise by how it moves, not by its face.

Rationale

Gesture treats Plover as a calligraphic mark that behaves like a bird: one or two smooth strokes that perch, sway, stretch, hop and loop. With no face to read, every state has to be legible as posture. A steady sway means "I'm here with you", a hop toward the screen means "come back", a tucked head means "off". That keeps the character calm and premium enough to sit next to real work for hours, while the small bird habits (wing-shakes, double nods, a happy loop) give it the warmth of a pet. All three variants share one motion vocabulary, so the eight states feel the same whichever body wins.

How I'd build it for real

Rive, with this prototype as its spec. Each variant here is a small parametric rig (position, tilt, squash, wing lift, head nod, tuck) driven by one pose function per state, and switching states blends from the current pose to the new one. That maps directly onto a Rive state machine with blend states and numeric inputs. The engineering side then sets state and Rive handles interruptible transitions, at about a 60 KB runtime. Lottie can't blend between arbitrary mid-loop poses. Hand-rolled SVG, as in this file, is a workable fallback because the rigs are tiny.
